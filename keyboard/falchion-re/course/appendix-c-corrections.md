# Appendix C: Every correction, and the lesson it teaches

The investigation keeps its mistakes on purpose. When something turned out wrong, a later log recorded the correction and the original stayed as it was. This appendix collects those corrections from [`TIMELINE.md` → "Corrections retained for auditability"](../TIMELINE.md) and the "corrected by log N" notes in [`FINDINGS.md`](../FINDINGS.md), orders them the way the course does, and gives each one its **moral**: the habit that would have prevented it.

[Lesson 19](19-mistakes-are-data.md) tells the story of these mistakes. This appendix is the reference list.

## The ten morals

Nearly every correction below teaches one of these ten habits:

| # | Moral | In one line |
|---|---|---|
| M1 | **The environment isn't the device** | A tool failing inside a sandbox tells you about the sandbox, not the keyboard. |
| M2 | **Check your parser before your conclusion** | A "difference" from a sloppy parser is a bug in the parser. |
| M3 | **Know where your evidence came from** | A file you can't trace to its source isn't evidence. |
| M4 | **Say exactly what you showed** | "Reproducible offline" isn't "boots". "Not found" isn't "absent". |
| M5 | **Read the code, not the shape** | Bytes that *look like* pointers or tables may be something else. |
| M6 | **A search is only as good as what it can see** | If a search can't see a shape, "no hits" means nothing. |
| M7 | **Test the test** | A check that can't fail proves nothing. |
| M8 | **Count carefully** | Off-by-one, test-before-increment, and "two" that was really "one". |
| M9 | **Names aren't identity** | A task name, a label, an address, and a bus address each mean less than they seem to. |
| M10 | **A safety tool must fail safe** | A tool that claims success it didn't check is worse than no tool. |
