  180053ca  eb00 0080   add.w  r0,r0,r0,lsl #2    x5

  180053d0  4342        muls   r2,r0,r2           parameter x 10 x multiplier
  180053d2  f85c 0024   ldr.w  r0,[r12,r4,lsl #2] a per-key 32-BIT COUNTER
  180053d6  4282        cmp    r2,r0              has the counter reached it?

and the counter is a TICK counter, incremented once per invocation:

  18005488  f858 3024   ldr.w  r3,[r8,r4,lsl #2]
  1800548c  1c5b        adds   r3,r3,#0x1
  1800548e  f848 3024   str.w  r3,[r8,r4,lsl #2]

The other three sites differ only in register allocation:
  180054ac  f89c c002 / eb0c 0c8c / ea4f 0c4c / fb01 f10c
  18005534  7880      / eb00 0080 / 0040      / 4343
  180055a2  f89c c002 / eb0c 0c8c / ea4f 0c4c / fb00 f00c

SO THE MULTIPLIER IS A SCALE FACTOR ON A TICK-DENOMINATED PER-KEY TIMEOUT. It is
not a loop bound, not a divisor, not a compare constant, not a timer reload
value and not a mailbox argument. Those were the five candidates the prompt
listed; this is the sixth, and it is the one that makes a stored user parameter
independent of the tick rate.

WHERE THE PARAMETER LIVES, read off the address arithmetic rather than guessed:

  18005492  ldr    r2,[r0,#0x8]           the layer
  18005494  muls   r2,r1,r2               r1 = 0x361
  18005496  ldr    r1,[pc]  -> 0x180202d8
  18005498  add.w  r1,r1,r2,lsl #2        + layer * 0x361 * 4
  1800549c  add.w  r2,r1,r4,lsl #5        + key * 0x20

  WHAT IS NOT CLAIMED. Log 125 mapped record fields +0x04..+0x1c. +0x20 and

STEP 4 — THE PERIOD, AND WHY THE SLOW STATE IS THE INFORMATIVE ONE
================================================================================
THE TRAP THIS AVOIDS. At index 3 log 126 measured the report grid at 125 us. It
is tempting to read that as the tick period — and it proves nothing, because the
host polls EP 0x81 every 125 us anyway (bInterval 1 at high speed, unchanged
across the whole capture). A 125 us completion grid there is equally explained by
the host's own schedule.

THE INDEX-0 MEASUREMENT IS THE ONE THAT CANNOT BE EXPLAINED AWAY. Log 126
measured the grid at 1 ms — 85.1% of inter-report gaps within 50 us of a
millisecond multiple against a 10% chance level, on EP 0x81, and 82.6% on the
independent EP 0x8c. The host was polling at 125 us throughout, so a 1 ms grid
must come from the DEVICE having new data only every millisecond.

  the gate says index 0 runs the report stage EVERY EIGHTH TICK
  the wire says index 0 makes new data available EVERY 1 ms
  therefore  8 x T = 1 ms  ->  T = 125 us  ->  IRQ38 = 8000 Hz

AND THE FAST STATE IS THEN A PREDICTION, NOT A FIT. Divisor 1 at 125 us predicts
a 125 us grid. Log 126 measured 125 us. The tool computes the prediction from the
derived period and a check requires it to match, so the model cannot be tuned to
agree.

CONFIDENCE: STRONGLY-INFERRED, and the reason is stated rather than buried. The
gate and the divisor are read out of the instruction stream — observed. The 1 ms
device grid is measured on the wire — observed. Combining them additionally
assumes THE REPORT STAGE MAKES AT MOST ONE NEW REPORT AVAILABLE PER INVOCATION,
which is not separately proven. That assumption is the whole gap, it is recorded
in the model as `kind_basis`, and a test forbids upgrading the confidence to
observed. It WOULD BE REFUTED BY a report stage that coalesced or emitted more
than one report per invocation, or by a second ungated producer for EP 0x81.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT RESOLVE, stated up front because it is the easy overclaim:
THE CLOCK CONFIGURATION. No crystal value, no PLL multiplier, no divider
register is recovered by this step. One derived period is not a register map.
The dependency map's `clock_frequency` service STAYS UNRESOLVED, and its
evidence boundary was refined to say so — and deliberately does NOT state the
number, because that map must not become the place a frequency is claimed. The
number lives in notes/polling-rate-reader.json. A test asserts both halves.

WHAT DOES NOW HAVE UNITS, for the first time from a measured clock rather than
by construction:

  IRQ38                    125 us / 8000 Hz          strongly-inferred, here
  the tick job at index 0  1 ms                      derived
  the tick job at index 3  125 us                    derived
  the per-key parameter    10 ms PER UNIT            derived, and RATE-INVARIANT

THE RATE-INVARIANCE IS THE CORROBORATION THAT MATTERS.

  index  divisor  multiplier  job period  invocations/unit  real time/unit
    0       8         1          1 ms           10             10 ms
    3       1         8         125 us          80             10 ms

  multiplier x divisor is 8 in both reachable states, so `parameter x 10 x
  multiplier` job invocations is THE SAME REAL DURATION at either rate. Two
  unrelated code sites — a gate in the entry image and a multiply in the
  application — agree on one rate model, and neither was used to derive the
  other. That is why this reads as the designer's intent rather than as a
  coincidence, and it is what makes the multiplier's purpose legible at all.

================================================================================
STEP 6 — WOULD 2000 AND 4000 Hz WORK?
================================================================================
THE QUESTION: the handler at 0x18002b2e accepts only index 0 and 3. If its two
compares at 0x18002b30 and 0x18002b34 were patched to accept 1 and 2, would 2000
and 4000 Hz work?

THE ANSWER: NO. And the reason needed the reader's semantics, which is why this
could not be answered before.

  1. THE PERIOD IS NOT SET BY THE MULTIPLIER. It is set by the gate, and the
     gate is `cmp r1,#3` — a TWO-WAY BRANCH. Index 1 or 2 fails it and takes the
     divide-by-eight path, so the report stage would still run at the 1000 Hz
     cadence. The rate would not change.

  2. AND THE TIMING WOULD BREAK. The multiplier WOULD become 2 or 4, so
     FUN_18004a7e would scale every per-key timeout to 20 or 40 job invocations
     per unit while the job period stayed at the 1000 Hz value. Hold and tap
     timings would come out 2x and 4x too long. The device would be worse than
     before, in a way a user would feel and a wire capture would not show.

  IF THE MULTIPLIER HAD BEEN AN INDEX INTO A TWO-ENTRY TABLE the answer would
  also have been no, but for a different reason. It is not: `1 << index` then
  `x10` is generic arithmetic with no table anywhere. That distinction is what
  the prompt asked for, and it cuts the opposite way from the obvious reading —
  the generic multiplier is the part that WOULD work; the gate is the part that
  would not.

WHAT WOULD ACTUALLY BE REQUIRED, as analysis. NOTHING WAS PATCHED.

  (a) the dispatcher's two compares at 0x18002b30/0x18002b34, so the device
      accepts the index at all; AND
  (b) the divider selection in the ENTRY image's tick job at 0x4e0/0x4ec,
      replaced by a rate-dependent divisor.

  A DIFFERENT IMAGE. (b) is in Candidate A, not the application — so this is a
  two-image change, not a two-compare change, and Candidate A is the image the
  bootloader selects and verifies. That is a materially different risk class
  from patching the application.

  THE ARITHMETIC ALREADY GENERALISES, and this is the genuinely informative part:
  divisor `8 >> index` pairs exactly with the existing multiplier `1 << index`.
  Their product is 8 for ALL FOUR indices, so the per-key parameter stays in
  10 ms units at 1000, 2000, 4000 and 8000 Hz with NO change to the application's
  scaling. ASUS'S SCALING IS ALREADY FOUR-RATE CAPABLE; only the gate is
  hardcoded to the two endpoints. That is consistent with log 126's independent
  finding that the stored field is four bits wide with a bound of 3, and it is
  the strongest available evidence that four rates were designed and two were
  shipped.

  NO DESCRIPTOR CHANGE IS NEEDED. EP 0x81's bInterval is already 1 — 125 us at
  high speed — so the host polls fast enough for any of the four rates without
  re-enumeration (log 126, wire-confirmed).

  NOT VERIFIABLE OFFLINE: CPU HEADROOM. At 2000 or 4000 Hz the gated stage would
  run two or four times more often than at 1000. Nothing offline measures whether
  the actuation compare, FUN_180045b6, FUN_180057fe and the report builder fit in
  the shortened budget. At 8000 Hz they demonstrably do, which bounds the
  question but does not answer it for the intermediate rates, since the sample
  fetch is already running every tick in all cases.

WHAT A CUSTOM FIRMWARE MUST REPRODUCE, from the gate's structure:

  - SAMPLE ON EVERY TICK. Veneer 0x4044 is called on BOTH paths. Hall
    acquisition is never slowed by the polling rate; only the decision and
    report stage is. A replacement that gates acquisition changes the sensor
    behaviour, not just the report rate.
  - GATE THE COMPARE AND THE REPORT TOGETHER. Veneers 0x4058, 0x4062, 0x406c and
    0x4076 are one block behind one test. Splitting them changes which stage
    sees which sample.
  - LEAVE THE 21-BYTE EP 0x8c SENDER UNGATED. Veneer 0x404e is a tail call on
    every tick regardless of the rate.
  - SCALE EVERY TICK-DENOMINATED TIMEOUT BY THE SAME FACTOR, or hold and tap
    timings move when the user changes the polling rate. The stock firmware does
    this with one cached byte and a x10.
  - KEEP bInterval AT 1. The host must poll faster than the fastest rate offered,
    and the descriptor never changes.

A CHECK THAT FAILED FOR THE RIGHT REASON, AND WAS NOT LOOSENED. Refining the
dependency map's clock boundary broke
test_map_platform_dependencies.test_no_frequency_is_claimed_anywhere, which bans
any `<number> Hz` from that map's report. The test was RIGHT: log 113 and log 124
put it there so the dependency map could never become the place a frequency gets
claimed. The boundary text was rewritten to point at
notes/polling-rate-reader.json for the number instead of stating it, and a NEW
test was added here asserting that division of responsibility in both directions
— the boundary must cite log 127 and this model, and must contain no numeral
followed by Hz. The existing test was not touched.

WHAT THIS CHANGES IN THE PRIOR RECORD
================================================================================
LOG 124 — REFINED, NOT REWRITTEN. Its residual negative, "no consumer of a rate
index appears in the preserved images", is now ANSWERED. Its `8000/8 = 1000`
alignment, recorded as "a consistency, not a measurement", is CONFIRMED as a
measurement. Its elimination of the PRESCALER LADDER stands exactly as written:
FUN_0000042c's five divisors really are instruction-stream immediates and no
stored index changes them. The rate does not change a divisor — IT BYPASSES A
DIVIDER, in FUN_000004ba, a function log 124 did not examine. Log 124's own tool
and tests are untouched and remain its sealed record.

LOG 126 — TWO CORRECTIONS, BOTH MINE TO MAKE. Its "six writers, no reader"
stands as a true statement about the three searches it ran; the reader existed
and the byte shape is why it was invisible. And its attribution of the sixth
writer to FUN_18007030 is WRONG: 0x18007a1e is in code with no function body.

LOGS 110 AND 119 — REFINED. "The actuation comparison runs on IRQ38's tick
divided by 8" is true at 1000 Hz and false at 8000 Hz, where it runs on every
tick. Both logs described the else path, which is the path the device takes at
its default rate, and both explicitly scoped the claim to "inside the branch
gated by that job's own /8 counter" — which is precisely the branch the rate
bypasses.

LOG 125 — ONE FIELD ATTRIBUTED. The per-key byte the multiplier scales lives in
its keymap bank, at layer*0xd84 + key*0x20 + 0x22, outside the +0x04..+0x1c range
it mapped. The field is NOT named here.

THE DEPENDENCY MAP — ONE BOUNDARY REFINED, CLASSIFICATION UNCHANGED.
`clock_frequency` stays unresolved. Its boundary previously read "Any Hz figure
would have to come from the silicon or from measurement, neither of which this
project has." The project now HAS a measurement, and the boundary says so while
pointing at this model for the number.


================================================================================
UNRESOLVED BOUNDARIES
================================================================================
  - THE ONE-REPORT-PER-INVOCATION ASSUMPTION behind IRQ38 = 8000 Hz. Not
    separately proven. It is the whole difference between strongly-inferred and
    observed here.
  - THE CLOCK CONFIGURATION. Crystal, PLL, and the reset sequence's registers
    are still unrecovered. A derived period is not a register map.
  - THE +0x20 / +0x22 RECORD FIELDS. Outside log 125's map, and not named. The
    behaviour on expiry — a halfword enqueued into a per-group ring — reads as a
    hold-time emit, and which HAL feature it is (ModTap, Toggle, DKS, SpeedTap)
    is not established.
  - CPU HEADROOM AT 2000 AND 4000 Hz. Not measurable offline.
  - WHAT GATES THE WHOLE JOB. *(0x1801e810) must equal 1 or the block is skipped
    entirely. Twenty-six write sites across five functions set it; its role is
    not traced here.
  - THE NINE RAPID WRITES log 126 recorded at t=314.3..318.2 in the capture.
    Cause still undetermined; nothing here depends on them.
  - WHETHER THE PARAMETER IS USER-FACING. 10 ms per unit is derived, but no
    Armoury Crate field has been matched to it. Log 125's three unmatched rows
    are down to two; this is not a third match.
